# The one where the loan shark comes to the house

Hey car folks,

Short version: your wife is on the phone now, the man you borrowed six grand
from would like a word, and the guy buying your spare alternator has stopped
telling you the same story every single time. Version 0.74.0. Here is what
landed.

---

## She texts you, and you can read all of it

She has been in this game a long time and almost everything she said got
dropped into a shared DM list, one line at a time, underneath a follower farm
advert and four strangers telling you the truck looks great. If you were here
for the trucks you could genuinely have missed that she was a character.

Open the Social tab now and the first thing on the phone is a message banner
from her, the same shape as the one on the phone in your pocket. Tap it and you
get the whole thread: everything she has said to you across the entire build,
in order, with the week it landed.

She also had almost nothing to say before, which is a strange thing to admit
about your number one fan. Now she texts most weeks, and what she says depends
on what the week actually looked like from her side of the house:

- Broke week: "checked the account. i am not mad. i am asking, in a normal
  voice, what the plan is."
- Four nights running in the garage: "the kids drew you. you are in the garage
  in the drawing. i thought you should know that."
- Good week: "since we are apparently rich this week, i am getting the good
  coffee. this is not up for discussion."
- No reason at all: "found a socket in the washing machine. adding it to the
  collection on the windowsill."

There is no reply button. You do not text back. You go downstairs.

## The loan shark stops by

You could always borrow six thousand dollars from a man in a loud shirt. Until
now the only thing that ever happened was the money coming back out of your
paycheck whether you had it or not, which made him the safest lender in the
valley.

He does not take a partial payment any more, and he does not take an IOU. Come
up short and the first week you get a card under your wiper with a little smiley
face drawn on it in ballpoint. Come up short twice and he comes by the house.

It plays out across three cards. A very clean sedan at the curb and a big
friendly man walking up your driveway admiring the truck. Then the garage door
comes down, and you are looking at a quiet suburban street with some noise
coming out of it. Then the door goes back up.

Nothing is broken that a doctor would put a name to. But you are going to be
sitting down for a while, and your right leg has developed strong opinions about
the clutch.

And then she comes out with a bag of frozen peas and a kitchen chair, puts the
chair down in the middle of the garage, and does not say the thing she has every
right to say. What she says is "what time do we leave Saturday?"

For three weeks she drives you to the shows. Shows stay open the whole time. She
keeps it at fifty five and will not be talked out of it, so the trip actually
costs you less gas than when you drive it. Then she hands the keys back and is
visibly unimpressed about it, and your seat is set for somebody six inches
shorter and there is a radio station in the presets that was not there before.

## Dale makes you earn it now

Dale's regulars rate was meant to be a loyalty ladder you climbed over a couple
of seasons. It turned out you could book work orders back to back on a single
Saturday and walk out at the full twenty five percent off before the first job
was even finished.

His rate moves one step per week now, and only on a week you actually bring him
something. Twenty drop offs in one afternoon buys exactly what one does. The
trust still banks at the old speed so none of those visits are wasted, they just
pay out over the weeks he would realistically have taken to warm up to you. If
you were already doing it a job a week, nothing about your game changes.

---

## The marketplace has stopped repeating itself

Playtesters told us the buyers were saying the same things over and over, and
they were right, and it was worse than a small pool. Three of the buyer types
were hard wired to exactly one story each, so Parking Lot Pete did the same
10:30 cart return meet on every single sale he ever made.

There are seventy two storylines now, up from seven, and nothing is bolted to
anybody. A buyer with stories of his own still mostly gets one of them, because
that is what makes him a character, but never the same one twice in a row.

- Meets that move to the Shell on the bypass, or to a gravel turnout with a
  mailbox and no house
- A man who pays in a shoebox of ones and will not take the eight dollars back
- A trade for a riding mower that runs when it wants to
- A trade for four wheels, three of which are perfect and one of which has a
  story involving a curb, a wedding and a cousin
- A guy who brings a friend, and the friend has opinions
- A dog that gets into your truck and will not get out
- Somebody restoring his father's truck who has never done any of this and is
  learning it off videos at night
- A man buying back the exact part he sold you two years ago
- A guy who argues the price up because he looked it up and is not comfortable
  paying you that little

The one who will not stop asking questions asks three now, and the block button
is sitting right there next to the answer every single time. Answer all three
and he drives out, pays cash, rounds up, and tells half the swap you are the
only seller who answers his messages.

## Sponsors live on the lid of your box

Sixteen sponsors listed as sixteen identical rows is a spreadsheet, and most of
those rows were answering a question nobody asked, namely what happens at nine
hundred thousand followers.

Your sponsors are stickers on your toolbox lid now, slapped on at slightly wrong
angles, and every one you sign shows up on it. Under that is one line for what
they add up to in cash, prize money and parts. Then the next one you are chasing
with a bar and a distance. The whole board is still one tap away.

**Two things we found while adding that up.** If you had signed both media
sponsors, the game was quietly giving you the smaller follower boost and
ignoring the bigger one, so SpeedNet's thirty five percent never applied at all.
That is fixed. And the top of the sponsor ladder used to read "1000.0K"
followers, which is a number no human being has ever said out loud.

## A real gas gauge

The fuel tank was a progress bar. It is a gauge off a dash now: chrome bezel,
black face, mint scale, orange needle. Fill the tank and the needle sweeps up
and settles, and the glass glows while the pump runs. Run it down at a show and
it swings back the other way.

---

## Cars and Coffee got redrawn

The old one did not hold together if you looked at it. The cars were parked on
the curb rather than in the stalls, the painted lines fanned out instead of
running parallel, and there was a coffee cup the size of a car floating in the
storefront window.

It is the real plaza now: cream stucco under barrel tile, arcades of round
arches, clipped hedges, a patio umbrella out on the walk, and people standing
around holding coffee, which is the half of the event the old picture did not
have at all. Your truck is parked in the aisle with a couple of neighbours
either side, at a size that actually makes sense next to them.

## Smaller things

- **Palomar Junction has a Main Street, not a downtown.** The bank the shark
  compares himself to, the pawn shop, the ring still sitting in its case: all up
  on Main now. The town is five streets deep. It was never going to have a
  downtown.
- **The Pamona swap meet.** Same swap, one letter different, for reasons our
  lawyer is happier about.
- **Your phone inbox is in order.** A sponsor offer that arrived this morning no
  longer sits underneath eight weeks of older messages.
- **Week one has no tab bar.** There is exactly one place to be, so the strip of
  screen holding a single button you cannot press has gone. It comes back the
  moment there is somewhere else to go.
- **Tool Storage means one thing.** There used to be a Tool Storage upgrade that
  gave you capacity, and a Tool Storage shelf in the shop selling a three hundred
  dollar tool chest that gave you none and ate a slot. That tool is called Trim
  and Fastener Bins now, which is what it actually does.
- **Seat covers and carpet look like seat covers and carpet** in the listing
  photos, instead of a brown block.

---

That is the push. As always: the truck is still yours, the money is still
imaginary, and the man in the loud shirt still has your number.
